# Sources: doc-writing bibliography

Provenance for the rules in `doc-writing-core.md`, `appendix-llm.md`, and `appendix-human.md`. Load this **only when revising the skill**; you don't need it to author a document. The sources below were extracted into tagged rules; this is the annotated reading list behind that extraction. When you add or change a rule, record its source and what you verified (read in full, abstract only, not read) here in the same change.

**Audience legend:** L = LLM-facing, H = human-facing, B = both.

## How the audience tags work

Source label doesn't decide the rule tag. A rule from an LLM-focused source can still be **[B]** if the underlying principle ("attention is scarce," "don't re-state what the audience already knows," "front-load the point," "gotchas are highest-value") holds for both audiences. During extraction a re-tag sweep moved ~40 rules from [L] → [B] on this basis.

Default to **[B]** when in doubt. Reserve **[L]** for rules that depend on model-specific mechanics (routing via description, progressive skill loading, tool_search, training cutoff) and **[H]** for rules that depend on human-specific mechanics (F-pattern scanning, reading age, accountability, review workflows).

### Authority sources
- [H] **[How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)**. Jakob Nielsen, 1997 (foundational). Scanning, subheads, bulleted lists, one idea per paragraph.
- [B] **[How Chunking Helps Content Processing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chunking/)**. Anna Kaley, 2019. Cognitive-load rationale for scannable chunks; applies to LLM comprehension too.
- [H] **[Top 10 tips for Microsoft style and voice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/top-10-tips-style-voice)**. Prescriptive enforceable rules.
- [H] **[Google developer documentation style: Voice and tone](https://developers.google.com/style/tone)** (+ [full guide](https://developers.google.com/style)). Conversational, concise, consistent terminology.
- [H] **[Writing for GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design/writing-for-gov-uk)**. Reading age, sentence length, front-loading the point.
- [H] **[Google developer documentation style: Write for a global audience](https://developers.google.com/style/translation)**. Primary source for `global-audience.md`: helper words and relative pronouns kept in, redundancy that buys clarity, primary-sense words, conditional-clause-first order, term consistency for machine translation, idiom/humor/culture/season bans. Paired with its [Contractions](https://developers.google.com/style/contractions) page, which recommends negation contractions because scanners drop a standalone "not."
- [H] **[ISO 24495-1:2023 Plain Language, Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines](https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html)**. The standards-body framing for plain language. Aimed at general-public documents but explicitly scoped to cover technical writing and controlled languages. Cite it when a review wants a standard to point at; the Google guide is what you actually write from.
- [H] **[Plain language guidelines (plainlanguage.gov)](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/)**. Audience analysis, active voice, short sentences, verbs over nominalizations.
- [B] **[ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English)** (Issue 9, January 2025). Controlled English for aerospace maintenance docs: 53 writing rules plus a ~900-word approved dictionary, free from [asd-ste100.org](https://asd-ste100.org/). Source of the procedural rules in `doc-writing-core.md` (20-word steps, one instruction per sentence, simple tenses, three-word noun clusters, one word one meaning). The dictionary is deliberately not adopted; the article's misapplication section explains why.
- [H] **[Purdue OWL: Conciseness](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/conciseness/index.html)**. Canonical "eliminating wordiness" checklist.
- [L] **[Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)** (2024-12-19). Simple composable patterns.
- [L] **[Anthropic: Equipping agents with Agent Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills)**. Canonical on Skill structure.
- [L] **[Anthropic: Claude Code best practices](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-best-practices)**. CLAUDE.md, tool definitions, agent instructions.
- [L] **[Anthropic: How Claude remembers your project (Memory)](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/memory)**. Official rules for CLAUDE.md structure, imports, length.
- [B] **`avoid-ai-writing`** (local skill), added mid-extraction. The puff/filler/tell lists incorporated into `doc-writing-core.md`.

### Audience by role (source for `audience-by-role.md`)
Verification status is stated per source. "Read in full" means the text was downloaded and the quoted phrases were found in it.
- [H] **[Commons Library: The Power of Story: The Story of Self, Us and Now](https://commonslibrary.org/the-power-of-story-the-story-of-self-us-and-now/)**. Read in full. A Leading Change Network guide to Marshall Ganz's public narrative, not Ganz's own text. Supports: a story of self communicates the values that called you to leadership; a story of us, the values shared by those in action; a story of now, an urgent challenge to those values that demands action now.
- [H] **[Grant, Campbell, Chen, Lapedis, and Lee (2007), Impact and the art of motivation maintenance](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/GrantCampbellChenCottoneLapedisLee_ImpactAndArt.pdf)**. Read in full. Field experiment: 17 callers in the contact condition spent about 10 minutes with one beneficiary, and Table 1 shows their mean donations rising from 185.94 to 503.22 while neither control group differed significantly from its own baseline. The percentages "142%" and "171%" that circulate in summaries do not appear in the paper. They match the Table 1 contact-group means (107.55 to 260.73 minutes; 185.94 to 503.22 donated) when computed as before-and-after change in that group alone. Persistence: the contact group's post mean differs significantly from the letter group's but not from the no-contact group's. The perceived-impact mediation test (Sobel z = 1.96) was in lab Experiment 2, not the field experiment.
- [H] **[Fernandes and Aires de Matos: Towards a better understanding of volunteer engagement](https://knowledge.unv.org/sites/default/files/2023-09/Towards%20a%20better%20understanding%20of%20volunteer%20engagement.pdf)**. Read in full. A survey of 450 volunteers grounded in self-determination theory. Supports the definition of autonomy, competence, and relatedness as the needs that underlie motivation types.
- **Not read:** [Steinmacher et al., The hard life of open source software project newcomers](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Igor_Steinmacher/publication/260592793_The_Hard_Life_of_Open_Source_Software_Project_Newcomers/file/6a85e531a75e158539.pdf) returned HTTP 403, and [arXiv 2101.10291 on open source contributor motivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.10291v3) was never fetched. Both were known only from search summaries, so no rule in `audience-by-role.md` relies on them. Read them before adding a rule about newcomer barriers or contributor motives.
- [H] **[Management Consulted: The Pyramid Principle Applied](https://managementconsulted.com/pyramid-principle/)**. Read in full. Supports leading with the recommendation ("Lead with the Assertion first"). Does not mention cost or deadlines.
- [H] **[Alfred: How to Write an Executive Summary That Gets Acted On](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/how-to-write-an-executive-summary)**. Read in full. Supports a one-sentence recommendation first, three key reasons, risks and assumptions, a deadline, and a stated investment amount. Written for executive summaries, not director requests.
- **Read but unused:** [HBR, How to Present to Senior Executives](https://hbr.org/2012/10/how-to-present-to-senior-execu) (Nancy Duarte, 2012) and [Lenny's Newsletter, How to communicate tradeoffs so leaders will listen](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-communicate-tradeoffs-so-leaders) (Tara Seshan, 2024) are paywalled. Only the free preview was readable, and it supports none of the rules, so neither is cited as support.
- **Engineer observations** ("Observations from a rewrite" in `audience-by-role.md`): no source. They come from one rewrite by the maintainer.

Director rules. These sources were fetched on 2026-10-26 by subagents that quoted them verbatim, and the quotes were checked against the saved files. None of them studies a request from one engineering team to another, so every mapping to the director rules is an inference.
- [H] **[Stanford Law School Policy Lab: Policy Memos guidelines (Luciana Herman)](http://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Policy-Memo-Guidelines-2.pdf)**. Read in full (7 pages; charts not recoverable as text). Guidance for students in a policy-writing workshop, adapted partly from Harvard Kennedy School. Supports: "For an option and decision memo, explain the pros and cons of each policy option," then "Identify which option will be recommended and which options will be discounted." Does not support leading with the recommendation: its "bottom line up front" is a statement of the problem. Has no wording on a decision deadline or on what else is affected. A search summary claimed it covered both, and the page does not.
- [H] **[Martin Stanley, Understanding the Civil Service: Submissions](https://civilservant.org.uk/skills-submissions.html)**. Read in full. This is a personal site by a former senior civil servant, not an official publication. He states that each department has its own structure. Supports: state the recommendation early ("It is important that you should recommend a specific course of action or decision"); a "Timing" section giving "the time scale within which the decision needs to be taken"; summarise the options with "the merits and demerits of each one." Has no wording on resource implications.
- [H] **[Frederick, Novemsky, Wang, Dhar, and Nowlis (2009), Opportunity cost neglect](https://doi.org/10.1086/599764)**, Journal of Consumer Research 36(4). Abstract only: the full text is paywalled, and the free copies were behind bot checks. The abstract says consumers "must actively generate the alternatives that it would displace" and "often fail to do so," and that cues to consider opportunity costs reduce purchase rates. The DVD and iPod numbers and the "tightwads were less affected" point come from [a Yale SOM news item](https://som.yale.edu/news/2009/05/opportunity-costs-remind-consumers-about-savings), a secondary source. The studies are about consumer purchases. Applying them to a director weighing a request is an inference.
- [H] **[Behavioural Insights Team, EAST: Four simple ways to apply behavioural insights](https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/BIT-EAST-handbook.pdf)**. Read in full (65 pages). Supports: "Even apparently small features that make a task more effortful can have a disproportionately large effect on behaviour." Examples with numbers: Quick Enrol "tripled participation rates among new employees," and a reduction in the effort to apply to university raised application rates for low-income students "from 26% to 68%." The handbook's lesson "Be specific about recommended actions" supports the exact-decision rule. The examples reduce the effort of the person acting, so reading them as support for offering a smaller first step is an inference.
- [H] **[US Army, AR 25-50: Preparing and Managing Correspondence](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/dr_pubs/dr_a/arn42124-ar_25-50-007-web-13.pdf)** (10 October 2020, administrative revision 4 October 2024). Read in full through a home-made text extractor, so a "not found" is weaker than a "found." Supports: "Two essential requirements include putting the main point at the beginning of the correspondence (bottom line up front)"; "Put the recommendation, conclusion, or most important information (the main point) next"; "Use a suspense date if a reply is needed by a certain date. Do not impose a suspense date without a compelling reason." It has no wording on options, courses of action, or advantages and disadvantages, though a search summary said Army decision papers include them. That summary cited a template site, not the regulation. The regulation's decision-memorandum body says only to include "the purpose, recommendations, discussion, and coordination."
- **Fetch failed:** the [Armor Writer's Guide](https://www.lineofdeparture.army.mil/Journals/Armor/Armor-Writers-Guide/) returned HTTP 403 (an access-denied page), so nothing from it is used.
- **Searched, not read:** two meta-analyses known only from a search summary, [Feeley, Anker, and Aloe (2012) on the door-in-the-face technique](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263263459_The_Door-in-the-Face_Persuasive_Message_Strategy_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_First_35_Years) and [Andrews, Carpenter, Shaw, and Boster (2008) on "even a penny will help"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230584128_The_Legitimization_of_Paltry_Favors_Effect_A_Review_and_Meta-Analysis). The summary reported that the door-in-the-face effect on actual behavior was not significant, and neither study is about asking a colleague. Neither is cited as support.
- **Not used:** template and blog pages that a search returned for decision memos (hogonext, heyjoyful, humanbees, modeldiplomat, climbtheladder, and a consultancy post on cross-team capacity). They were never fetched, and several repeat each other.
